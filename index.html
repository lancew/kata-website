What is Judo Kata Tournament Manager?

Judo Kata Tournament Manager is browser-based software that runs an entire judo kata competition from one laptop on the venue’s local network. It handles the draw, judge tablets, live hall results, the announcer board, printable PDF scoresheets, and CSV exports, with no internet connection required.

Why it helps

For organisers

  • Set the event name, logo, included kata, and scoring rules once in Config
  • Add competitors and judges by hand, import them from CSV, or pull competitors from this year’s IJF kata events on Judobase
  • Run the draw, seat the panel, and assign performances to mats
  • Start the final when prelims are complete — re-draw advancers, re-seat judges, and keep prelim scores on record
  • Print a full-day PDF pack — or one kata — when you need paper at the table
  • Lock clerk pages with a PIN, including Config and the scoring audit; share public links for tablets and projectors
  • Keep each event in its own SQLite file; no internet required on the day

For judges

  • Open a seat URL that shows your name, the kata, and tori and uke on the mat
  • Tap small, medium, and big mistakes, forgotten techniques, and ± corrections — each tap is saved at once
  • Submit when the kata finishes to lock the seat; the tablet waits for the rest of the panel
  • Sit as shadow to record a score that is stored and ignored in the total
  • Use dark mode and zoom under hall lighting; the clerk can unsubmit if you sent too soon

For the hall

  • Put the announcer board on a hall screen: current tori and uke, judging team, who is on deck
  • Project live results that update as judges tap, with provisional points and an in-progress marker, and can rotate through kata
  • Run the pre-event draw ceremony and final medal award ceremony on the same hall screen, controlled from a tablet with synced audio and animation
  • Scale the hall table to fit every pair from rank 1 on a 1080p screen; show who advances during prelims
  • Set the live screens to English, French, or German in Config
  • Spectators can use public links without the organiser PIN

Scoring you can trust

Choose EJU, kata-judge/BJA-style, or IJF in Config. The same tablet marks produce the event total — forgotten techniques, drop rules, and tie-breaks included — without rebuilding a spreadsheet at the venue.

It follows Michel Kozlowski’s Excel Kata Scoresheets (EJU) — the reference many EJU organisers still use — and is dedicated to his memory.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

The software is free and open source under the AGPL. Demos and supervised test events are open now; a public download isn’t available yet — get in touch and we’ll set you up. On-site support is optional and costs £100 per day plus travel.

What happens if the venue has no internet?

Nothing — it doesn’t need any. The system runs on a laptop on the local network. If a live connection drops, the affected pages reload every five seconds.

Will the scoring match our ruleset?

Choose EJU, kata-judge/BJA-style, or IJF in Config. The rulesets were validated against the senior categories of the 2024 Silesian Open Judo Kata Competition.

What hardware do I need?

One laptop acts as the server; judges, the announcer, and displays use any browser on the same network. Optional Docker packaging is available for consistent deployment.

What if a judge’s tablet fails?

The event keeps running. The mat clerk can enter the same scores on a browser scoresheet, and every tap is saved as it happens, so nothing is lost.

Where is our data kept?

Each event is a single SQLite file on your own machine. There is no cloud service and no account to create.

Which kata are supported?

Nage-no-Kata, Katame-no-Kata, Ju-no-Kata, Kime-no-Kata, Kodokan Goshin Jutsu, Koshiki-no-Kata, Itsutsu-no-Kata, and Kodomo-no-Kata 1–7. See the scoring page for details.
